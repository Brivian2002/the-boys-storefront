# Marketplace Discovery Research — La Glitz

This private design note records public marketplace patterns studied for the La Glitz redesign. It is a source of interaction principles, not a visual template. La Glitz will retain its own luxury palette, typography, product presentation, and single-store identity.

| Marketplace | Public pattern observed | La Glitz interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Jiji](https://jiji.ng/) | A search-first entry point, a visible category rail, and dense direct listing cards make browsing immediate. Cards surface the deciding facts before a listing is opened. | Put search, jewelry categories, and concise product facts above editorial storytelling. Retain calm spacing and luxury imagery rather than adopting classified-listing density. |
| [AliExpress](https://www.aliexpress.com/index.html) | Category shortcuts, promotional discovery modules, and compact product tiles reduce the time from intent to item. | Use seasonal discovery and collection modules only when Blogger products exist; every tile should lead to a product page or checkout, never a blog post. |
| [Amazon customer help](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GSUNWNFT2ALMPR3L) | Amazon describes product discovery through a search box or departmental browsing, then result sorting and filtering. | Keep one high-priority search action, an understandable jewelry hierarchy, and concise refinements. Do not import marketplace clutter, third-party seller concepts, or review signals. |
| [eBay product research help](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/selling-tools/product-research?id=4853) | eBay describes keyword-led discovery supported by filters such as category, condition, brand, color, and location. | Allow the catalog to expose useful Blogger-defined attributes as controlled facets where there are values to browse; never fabricate ratings, reviews, or sales signals. |

The proposed La Glitz system has three layers: a product search and category layer; a filterable catalog grid with price, material, collection, availability, and Blogger-defined attributes; and a product detail page with directly actionable shopping controls. Blogger remains the server-side catalog source. The protected Atelier translates dashboard fields into structured Blogger labels and product content. Direct visual access to Amazon and eBay was unavailable in the research browser, so their publicly accessible help documentation, rather than scraped layouts, informs these interaction principles.

The management indicator will be a tiny, clearly non-shopper-control orange signal in the footer utility area. It opens `/atelier`, whose server-side password session remains the security boundary. The indicator must not reveal dashboard data, authentication state, or a customer-facing publishing workflow.
